Every year, many tax-saving investments are bought in the last two weeks of March, in a hurry, to meet a deadline. Equity-linked savings schemes (ELSS) deserve a more deliberate place in a plan.

## What ELSS is

An ELSS is an equity mutual fund that qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act under the old tax regime, within the overall 80C limit. Each investment is locked in for three years from its date of purchase. That is the shortest lock-in among the common 80C options.

## Check your regime first

The 80C deduction applies only if you file under the old tax regime. If the new regime suits you better, ELSS has no tax benefit for you, and it should be judged as any other equity fund.

## Why March buying is a poor habit

- **The whole year's amount goes in at once**, at whatever the market level happens to be in March.
- **Liquidity suffers**: a large lump locked in for three years, often from money that was needed elsewhere.
- **Fund choice is rushed**: the decision is made on a deadline, not on fit.

A monthly SIP into ELSS from April spreads the purchase across the year. Remember that each SIP instalment has its own three-year lock-in.

## Fit it into the plan

Count ELSS towards a long-term equity goal, such as retirement, rather than treating it as a separate tax product. If your 80C limit is already filled by EPF, life cover premiums, PPF or home-loan principal, extra ELSS gives no further deduction.

Tax rules change. Confirm your position with a tax professional before investing.
