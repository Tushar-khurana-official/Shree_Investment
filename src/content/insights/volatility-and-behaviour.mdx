Every few years, Indian equity markets fall sharply. Each time, it feels different, and each time the most expensive mistakes are behavioural rather than analytical.

## Volatility is the price of equity returns

Equity has historically earned more than fixed deposits over long periods precisely because it is uncomfortable to hold. Prices swing, sometimes by 20% to 40% within months. If equity were smooth, there would be no reason for it to pay more. Volatility is not a sign that something is broken; it is the fee you pay for the higher expected return.

## The three costly reactions

1. **Stopping SIPs after a fall.** This stops buying exactly when units are cheapest, and removes the averaging benefit that makes SIPs work.
2. **Switching to last year's winner.** Chasing the best recent performer often means buying high after the move has happened.
3. **Moving everything to "safe" assets and waiting for clarity.** Clarity tends to arrive after prices have already recovered.

## What helps

- **Match money to its date.** Money needed soon should not be in equity in the first place, so a fall does not force a sale.
- **Keep an emergency fund.** Six months of expenses in liquid instruments means a job loss does not become a forced redemption.
- **Write the rule down before you need it.** A plan that says "we keep SIPs running and rebalance at a five-point drift" is easier to follow than a decision made in a falling market.

## The role of a review

A good review in a falling market is often short. It checks that each goal's money is still in the right place for its date, confirms the SIPs, and changes nothing else. Doing nothing, deliberately, is frequently the most valuable advice there is.
